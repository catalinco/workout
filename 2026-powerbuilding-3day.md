# Powerbuilding 3-Day — 12 Weeks for Back, Chest & Glutes

**Goal:** Strength and bodybuilding alongside iaido twice and tango twice weekly.  
**Duration:** Three four-week blocks (approximately three months).  
**Gym days:** Monday, Wednesday, Friday.  
**Excluded:** Leg curls and calf raises.

## Monthly emphasis

| Block | Weeks | Accessory focus | Planned lighter week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Month 1 | 1–4 | Back: vertical pulls, supported rows, straight-arm pulldowns | 4 |
| Month 2 | 5–8 | Chest: incline press, flat press, pec deck | 8 |
| Month 3 | 9–12 | Glutes: hip thrusts, kickbacks, plus existing lunges and RDLs | 12 |

Deadlift, overhead press, and squat remain the main strength lifts throughout. Accessory volume shifts toward the priority area while other areas retain work. Specialization does not mean training only one muscle group.

Normal-week direct back work totals 14 sets in Month 1 and 6 in Months 2–3, counting rows and pulldowns but not deadlifts or rear-delt flyes. Direct chest work totals 4, 11, and 4 sets respectively. Month 3 adds 5 hip-thrust sets and 2 kickback sets weekly; these are additional glute-focused sets, not a count of all glute work from squats, lunges, and hinges. Monday's light squats are removed in Month 3 to make room.

These are starting volumes, not guaranteed maintenance or growth thresholds. Do not add more sets automatically.

## Reading and ordering workouts

- All listed sets are working sets; preparation sets are additional.
- `2x4-6` means two sets of four to six reps. Unilateral reps are per leg or side.
- RIR means reps in reserve: 2 RIR means about two more clean reps would be possible.
- Pick pull-ups or lat pulldowns for each slot and retain that choice throughout a block. Adjust assistance or added load as needed.
- Walking lunges may replace reverse lunges using the same sets and reps per leg.
- Perform the main strength lift first. Then prioritize that month's focus exercises: back pulls/rows in Month 1, chest presses/pec deck in Month 2, hip thrusts in Month 3. Finish with remaining accessories; kickbacks come after compound work.
- The app retains a fixed row order across months. Inactive exercises show “Rest”; skip them. The tables below omit inactive exercises.

## Progression within each month

### First week — Weeks 1, 5, 9

Choose loads near the bottom of the rep range with about 3 RIR. For retained exercises, use the last successful working weights as a reference, reducing them if needed. When set counts increase, do not also force a weight increase. Start new exercises conservatively.

### Second and third weeks — Weeks 2–3, 6–7, 10–11

- Add reps within the range while keeping about 2 RIR on compound lifts and 1–2 RIR on isolation work. Keep Pallof presses controlled with 2–3 good reps available.
- Keep Monday's light squats at 3–4 RIR and Friday's RDLs at 2–3 RIR.
- Keep hip thrusts and lunges at 2–3 RIR during the glute block; do not chase soreness before practice.
- Once every set reaches the top of its range with the intended reserve and sound technique, increase the weight by the smallest practical increment and return toward the bottom of the range.
- Typical barbell increments are 1–2.5 kg for upper-body lifts and 2.5–5 kg for lower-body lifts. Use smaller or equipment-appropriate increases as needed.
- Example: squat 5/5/4, then 5/5/5, eventually 6/6/6, then increase weight. These steps need not happen in consecutive weeks.
- Repeated rep ranges in the JSON are intentional. Progress by reps and load, not mandatory weekly increases in sets.

### Fourth week — Weeks 4, 8, 12

Use the reduced sets in the tables (roughly half, rounded up), lower working weights around 10–15%, use the bottom of the rep range, and keep at least 4 RIR. Reduce further if needed. No maximum testing is scheduled.

After Week 12, review lift progress, muscle-growth priorities, and practice quality before choosing the next block. Do not assume that each month's specialization requires an automatic weight increase.

## Warm-up and rest

- Begin with a few minutes of easy movement, then progressively heavier preparation sets for the main lift. Add preparation sets before other exercises as needed.
- Back extensions are optional: one easy set if useful, without tiring the lower back.
- Rest 3–4 minutes for heavy lifts, about 2 minutes for other compound exercises, and 60–90 seconds for isolation work. Extend rest when needed for performance.

## Recovery adjustments

Track gym performance, soreness, and freshness during iaido and tango. The glute month intentionally raises local workload despite your already-developed legs, so adjust it to practice quality.

- Month 1: if back/grip fatigue affects sword practice, remove the straight-arm pulldowns first or reduce a pulling exercise by one set. Keep rows supported.
- Month 2: if shoulder or chest fatigue affects practice, reduce pec deck by one set and then a DB press by one set if needed.
- Month 3: if Monday work affects tango or Tuesday iaido, omit kickbacks. If Wednesday affects Thursday iaido, reduce lunges to one set or hip thrusts to two. If Friday affects tango, remove one RDL or hip-thrust set first.
- In Months 1–2, reduce Monday's light squats by one set if practice suffers.
- If recovery remains poor, bring the lighter week forward instead of forcing progression. Persistent pain calls for changing the provoking movement, not pushing through it.

## Basis

The combination of heavier strength work, moderate-rep hypertrophy work, and no obligatory failure training follows the broad principles in [ACSM's 2026 resistance-training guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/). The three monthly emphases, exact set counts, and scheduled deloads are practical programming choices for this schedule, not a claim that this rotation is superior to other approaches.
