# Powerbuilding 3-Day — 12 Weeks for Back, Chest & Glutes

**Goal:** Strength and bodybuilding alongside iaido twice and tango twice weekly.  
**Duration:** Three four-week blocks (approximately three months).  
**Gym days:** Monday, Wednesday, Friday.  
**Excluded:** Leg curls and calf raises.

## Monthly emphasis

| Block | Weeks | Accessory focus | Planned lighter week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Month 1 | 1–4 | Back: vertical pulls and supported rows | 4 |
| Month 2 | 5–8 | Chest: incline press, flat press, pec deck | 8 |
| Month 3 | 9–12 | Glutes: hip thrusts, kickbacks, plus existing Bulgarian split squats and RDLs | 12 |

Deadlift, overhead press, and squat remain the main strength lifts throughout. Accessory volume shifts toward the priority area while other areas retain work. Specialization does not mean training only one muscle group.

Working-week direct back volume is 12 sets in Month 1 and 6 in Months 2 and 3, counting rows and vertical pulls. Straight-arm pulldowns are omitted. Direct chest volume remains 4, 11, and 4 sets respectively. Month 3 keeps three Wednesday hip-thrust sets and two Monday kickback sets; Friday hip thrusts are omitted. These glute accessories are in addition to squats, deadlifts, RDLs, and Bulgarian split squats. Monday light squats remain omitted in Month 3.

Accessory set counts stay stable during each month's first three weeks. Only the priority accessories vary reps, while main lifts vary both sets and reps. This avoids increasing every accessory's workload at once.

## Weekly schedule

Gym sessions are in the morning. Each iaido session lasts two hours in the evening. The specific weekdays below follow your earlier notes and should be adjusted if your class timetable has changed.

| Day | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Morning gym Day 1 + tango |
| Tuesday | Evening iaido — 2 hours |
| Wednesday | Morning gym Day 2 |
| Thursday | Evening iaido — 2 hours |
| Friday | Morning gym Day 3 + tango |
| Saturday / Sunday | Recovery |

Aim for gym sessions of about 60 minutes, allowing longer when warm-ups or full rests require it. If time is limited, omit the last small isolation exercise rather than rushing heavy lifts. Separate gym and evening practice by several hours, with meals and rest between them. Morning/evening separation helps scheduling but does not eliminate accumulated fatigue.

## Reading and ordering workouts

- All listed sets are working sets; preparation sets are additional.
- `2x5` means exactly two working sets of five reps. Complete the prescribed reps in every set; there are no rep ranges or AMRAP sets. Unilateral reps are per leg or side.
- RIR means reps in reserve: 2 RIR means about two more clean reps would be possible.
- Pick pull-ups or lat pulldowns for each slot and retain that choice throughout a block. Adjust assistance or added load as needed.
- Bulgarian split squat reps are per leg. Choose a load appropriate to this exercise rather than automatically using your previous lunge weight.
- Perform the main strength lift first. Then prioritize that month's focus exercises: back pulls/rows in Month 1, chest presses/pec deck in Month 2, hip thrusts in Month 3. Finish with remaining accessories; kickbacks come after compound work.
- The app retains a fixed row order across months. Inactive exercises show “Rest”; skip them. The tables below omit inactive exercises.

## Fixed-weight progression

Keep each exercise's working weight unchanged within its four-week block, including the lighter week. Each exercise/day slot can have its own load; Monday and Friday pulling need not use the same load. All working sets within a slot use that load. Preparation sets use lighter weights.

- First exercise each day (deadlift, overhead press, back squat): **3x5, 4x4, 3x5, 2x3** across each block.
- The main-lift baseline is 3x5. Week 2 spreads 16 total reps across four shorter sets; Week 3 returns to 15 reps in three sets. This is a small workload variation, not an automatic weekly increase. Week 4 reduces work to six reps. Compare control and effort between Weeks 1 and 3 before considering a load increase for the next block.
- Priority accessories vary exact reps as shown in the tables, with no extra working sets. The second week uses shorter sets, the third week longer sets, and the fourth week less work.
- Supporting accessories keep their working-week sets and reps steady. The lighter week reduces their sets and reps.

### Choosing the weight and effort

For the main lifts, select a load that permits all three sets of five with at least two clean reps available on the last set, and maintain that reserve during Week 2's four sets of four. For priority accessories, choose the load with Week 3's longest sets in mind and leave at least two clean reps available on presses, rows, and pulldowns. Leave three on Bulgarian split squats, RDLs, and hip thrusts, and four on light squats. Earlier weeks may feel easier; do not increase weight to make every week equally hard. Keep two reps available on isolation work and Pallof presses. Deadlift volume is higher than in the previous version; choose its starting load conservatively so the third set stays controlled and evening practice is unaffected.

If Bulgarian split squats are unfamiliar, start conservatively and choose their load independently of the old lunge load. The fixed-weight rule is a plan, not a reason to force missed reps: reduce weight if technique or the intended reserve cannot be maintained. Do not compensate with extra reps or sets.

### Weeks 4, 8, and 12 — recovery

Keep the same load and perform only the reduced sets and reps listed. Aim for at least four clean reps in reserve. If the work still feels demanding or accumulated fatigue persists, reduce the load as needed. There is no scheduled maximum test or automatic percentage reduction.

### Between months

Review the last working week's performance and practice quality. Retain the load or use the smallest available increase only if every prescribed rep was completed with the intended reserve. Account for changes in accessory set count when choosing the next block's load. New exercises get their own conservative starting weights. Do not automatically increase weight every week or every month.

After Week 12, review performance, recovery, and priorities before choosing the next block.

## Warm-up and rest

- Begin with a few minutes of easy movement, then progressively heavier preparation sets for the main lift. Add preparation sets before other exercises as needed.
- Back extensions are optional: one easy set if useful, without tiring the lower back.
- Rest 3 minutes for heavy lifts, 2 minutes for other compound exercises, and 90 seconds for isolation work. Extend rest when needed for performance.

## Recovery adjustments

Track gym performance, soreness, and freshness during iaido and tango. The glute month intentionally raises local workload despite your already-developed legs, so adjust it to practice quality.

- Month 1: if back/grip fatigue affects sword practice, reduce a pulling exercise by one set; straight-arm pulldowns are already omitted. Keep rows supported.
- Month 2: if shoulder or chest fatigue affects practice, reduce pec deck by one set and then a DB press by one set if needed.
- Month 3: if Monday work affects tango or Tuesday iaido, omit kickbacks. If Wednesday affects Thursday iaido, reduce Bulgarian split squats to one set or hip thrusts to two. If Friday affects tango, remove one RDL set first; Friday hip thrusts are already omitted.
- In Months 1–2, reduce Monday's light squats by one set if practice suffers.
- If recovery remains poor, bring the lighter week forward instead of forcing progression. Persistent pain calls for changing the provoking movement, not pushing through it.

## Basis

The combination of heavier strength work, moderate-rep hypertrophy work, and no obligatory failure training follows the broad principles in [ACSM's 2026 resistance-training guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/). The three monthly emphases, fixed-weight waves, exact set counts, and scheduled deloads are practical programming choices for this schedule, not a claim that this rotation is superior to other approaches.
